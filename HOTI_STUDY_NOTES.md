## 1. Aim and rationale (pp. 1–2)

- **Background:**
  - Skin aging has **intrinsic** causes (genetics, cell senescence) and **extrinsic** ones (UV, pollution, lifestyle).
  - **Dermal fibroblasts** maintain the extracellular matrix, redox balance and inflammation/apoptosis signalling.
    When they are impaired, the skin loses **elasticity**, **TEWL increases** and visible aging appears.
  - **Polyphenols** are antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, photoprotective and anti-aging. Their efficacy depends on
    **chemical stability, bioavailability and interaction with the cosmetic vehicle**, so formulation design matters.
- **Sustainability angle:**
  - The industry is moving toward ingredients from **agri-food by-products**.
  - Brewing by-products are plentiful and underused. They contain phenolic acids, flavonoids, proteins,
    polysaccharides and minerals.
  - Darker, malt-rich beers have more phenolics.
  - The work fits **circular-economy** principles (p. 16).
- **Earlier work by the same group:** **Triplo Malto** had the highest phenolic content and antioxidant activity of
  the beers they tested, and craft-beer by-products showed anti-aging activity in keratinocytes.
- **Aim:** to explore the cosmetic potential of **Bionda Triplo Malto beer, its wort, and the by-products hops, yeast
  and spent grain** in **dermal fibroblasts**, and to see how **formulation** changes efficacy. They do this by
  combining **in vitro cell assays with in vivo (clinical) skin measurements**.
- **Figure 1 (p. 3), the study design:**
  1. Extraction of bioactive compounds.
  2. In vitro assays on immortalised human fibroblasts. The figure lists MTS (viability), TAC (antioxidant) and
     **Western blot** (pro- and anti-apoptotic proteins).
  3. Cosmetic emulsions are made and tested on fibroblasts.
  4. In vivo testing of **hydration, elasticity and TEWL**.

## 2. Materials and methods (pp. 3–7)

### Samples (2.1)

There are 5 samples, all from **Birrificio Artigianale Collesi** (Apecchio, Italy):

- **BB:** beer
- **W:** wort
- **H:** hops
- **Y:** yeast
- **T:** spent grain

The by-products are residues left after mashing and filtration. The **reference controls** for in vitro work are
**açai dry extract**, **Trolox** (antioxidants, 0.5 mg/mL) and **prostaglandin E2 (PGE2)** at 10⁻⁶ M.

### Cell model (2.2)

- **BJ-5ta**: immortalised human dermal fibroblasts (ATCC CRL-4001, male diploid).
- Grown in DMEM/MED 199 (3:1) with 10% fetal calf serum, penicillin and streptomycin, at 37 °C and 5% CO₂.
- Cells were split at 70–80% confluence.

### Aqueous extraction (2.3)

1. Wet solids and liquids were **freeze-dried (lyophilised)** at −50 °C and 0.03 bar. Dry solids were milled in a
   cutter mill.
2. Each sample was mixed with **water, 1 g in 10 mL**, and **magnetically stirred for 24 h** at room temperature.
3. It was **centrifuged** at 12,000 rpm, 20 °C, for 10 min, then freeze-dried again and stored at **−20 °C** in
   airtight vials.

- Material was **normalised to dry weight**.
- Extractions were done in independent replicates.
- **Why water and not ethanol (3.1):** earlier work showed ethanol extracts were not active enough to justify their
  much higher cost.

### MTS cell viability assay (2.4)

- **Principle:** metabolically active cells reduce a tetrazolium salt to coloured formazan, so the colour reflects
  viability and metabolic activity.
- **Set-up:**
  - 5 × 10³ cells per well in 96-well plates, treated for **24 h**.
  - **Free extracts** were tested at **0.25, 0.5 and 1 mg/mL**. Higher doses were avoided because they can change the
    culture medium and interfere with the assay.
  - **Emulsions** (20% w/w extract) were diluted to **10 mg/mL extract**, with **Emulsion WE** (no extract) as the
    vehicle control.
- **Reading:** MTS reagent (20 µL) for 1 h, then absorbance at **490 nm**. Results are % of untreated control
  (control = 100%).

### Total Antioxidant Capacity (TAC) assay (2.5)

- **Principle:** a BioVision K274-100 kit. Small antioxidant molecules reduce **Cu²⁺ to Cu⁺**, and protein interference
  is masked.
- **Oxidative stress:** **UVB lamp (peak 312 nm)** for **5 min**, at a non-lethal dose.
- **Reading:** after a 24 h incubation, absorbance is read at **570 nm** and expressed as **Trolox equivalents** using a
  calibration curve.
- **Check:** viability after UV was confirmed by MTS.

### Emulsions (2.6, Table 1)

There are **6 oil-in-water (O/W) emulsions**:

- **Emulsion WE**: the reference, with no active ingredient.
- **Emu1** beer, **Emu2** wort, **Emu3** hops, **Emu4** yeast, **Emu5** spent grain. Each contains **20% w/w dry
  extract**.

Only ranges are given for the ingredient amounts, because of industrial confidentiality.

| Phase | Ingredients (%) |
|---|---|
| **A (oil phase)** | Emulium Kappa 2 (emulsifier, 2–6), cetearyl alcohol (emulsifier, 2–5), shea butter (emollient/moisturising, 1–10), Cetiol LC coco-caprylate/caprate (emollient/emulsifier, 1–5), sunflower seed oil (emollient, 1–5) |
| **B (water phase)** | Water (solvent, q.b.), Avicel PC 591 microcrystalline cellulose + cellulose gum (thickener, 0.5–1.5), **extract 20% (active)**. INCI names: Humulus Lupulus extract, Saccharomyces Cerevisiae extract, Hordeum Vulgare seed extract, beer/wort extract |
| **C (preservative/chelating)** | Euxil K900 (benzyl alcohol, ethylhexylglycerin, tocopherol; preservative, 0.5–1), Natrlquest E30 (trisodium ethylenediamine disuccinate; chelator, 0.1–0.5) |

**Method:** phases A and B are heated to **70 °C** and mixed with a high-shear mixer (Ultra-Turrax T25). Phase C is
added on cooling at **40 °C**. The pH is adjusted to **6.0**, using **triethanolamine** after 24 h of stabilisation
(p. 7).

### Physicochemical stability (2.7)

- **Measured:** density (by weighing), pH (Jenway 3510 meter) and viscosity (Mettler Toledo viscometer).
- **Accelerated stability:** centrifugation at **4000 rpm, 30 min, 20 °C**, plus storage at **4 °C and 40 °C**.
- **Long-term stability:** room temperature for **up to 12 months**.
- **Colour:** measured with a Konica Minolta CR-400 colorimeter in **CIELAB**, using **ΔE\*ab = √(ΔL\*² + Δa\*² + Δb\*²)**.

### Microbiological challenge test (2.8)

The test follows **ISO 11930:2012, Criterion A**.

- **Microbes:** *E. coli*, *S. aureus*, *P. aeruginosa*, *C. albicans* and *A. brasiliensis*.
- **Sampling:** CFU counted on **days 0, 7, 14 and 28**.
- **Pass criteria:**
  - **Bacteria:** at least a **3-log reduction within 7 days**, with no increase on days 14 or 28.
  - **Yeasts and moulds:** at least a **1-log reduction within 14 days**, with no increase on day 28.
- **Starting amounts:** 10⁵–10⁶ CFU for the bacteria and 10⁴–10⁵ CFU for the fungi.

### In vivo studies (2.9)

- **Patch test for skin compatibility:**
  - Done on healthy volunteers following the **Declaration of Helsinki and COLIPA guidelines**.
  - Irritation was read **15 min and 24 h after the patch was removed**.
- **Efficacy study:**
  - **50 healthy volunteers**: **phototype III**, men and women, **age 27 ± 4 years**.
  - **30 days** of daily use, with measurements at **T0 (baseline)** and **T30**.
  - Measurements were taken under controlled environmental conditions using validated **non-invasive** instruments
    from **Delfin Technologies** (Kuopio, Finland):
    - **TEWL**: **VapoMeter®**
    - **Hydration**: **MoistureMeterSC®**
    - **Elasticity**: **ElastiMeter®**
- **Design:**
  - **Randomised split-forearm.** An **independent staff member** assigned products to forearm areas.
  - **Double-blind:** neither the volunteers nor the person taking measurements knew which product was where.
  - **Figure 2 (p. 6):** each forearm has 3 zones with **6 measurement points per zone**. For example, the right arm
    gets Ref, Emu1 and Emu2, and the left arm gets Ref, Emu3, Emu4 and Emu5.
- **Ethics (p. 17):**
  - written **informed consent**
  - supervision by a **dermatologist**
  - Declaration of Helsinki and COLIPA

### Statistics (2.10)

- **Software:** GraphPad Prism 9. Data are shown as **mean ± SD**.
- **In vitro:** **n = 3 independent experiments**, analysed by **one-way ANOVA** with post-hoc tests (**Dunnett's** vs
  one control, **Tukey's** for all pairs). The figure legends say two-way ANOVA (treatment × concentration) + Dunnett.
- **In vivo:** n = 50, analysed by **two-way repeated-measures ANOVA** (treatment × time, T0 vs T30). Each formulation
  was compared with Emulsion WE using a **Holm–Bonferroni** correction.
- **Significance level:** **p < 0.05**.
- **In vitro vs in vivo:** an exploratory **Spearman rank correlation**.

## 3. Results

### Extraction yield (3.1, p. 7)

| Sample | Yield |
|---|---|
| Beer | ≈97% |
| Wort | ≈97% |
| Hops | **78.0 ± 2.5%** |
| Yeast | **92.0 ± 5.3%** |
| Spent grain | **65.0 ± 3.4%** |

These yields mean the water-soluble polar compounds were recovered efficiently.

### Chemical profile (3.2, p. 7, published data from the same batches)

- **Methods used earlier:** Folin–Ciocalteu (total phenolics), **DPPH/ABTS/FRAP** (antioxidant capacity), and
  **HPLC–MS/MS** for 20 phenolic compounds.
- **Malt/wort:** malt phenolic acids, especially **trans-p-coumaric acid**, pass into the wort.
- **Hops:** bitter acids and prenylflavonoids (**humulone/cohumulone, lupulone/colupulone,
  xanthohumol/isoxanthohumol, 8-prenylnaringenin**). These are lower in spent hops.
- **Spent yeast:** **concentrates phenolics** from the brewing medium.
- **Link to results:** this is why the beer and yeast products worked best (fermentation phenolics and yeast
  bioactives).

### Free extracts, MTS (3.3, p. 8, Figure 3)

- At 0.25–1 mg/mL there was **no significant change in viability** for any extract.
- Beer and wort at 0.5 mg/mL gave a small rise that was **not significant**.
- **No cytotoxicity**, so the extracts are cytocompatible, but they had no direct effect on proliferation.
- **Limitation:** a single immortalised cell line does not reflect real skin. Primary fibroblasts, keratinocytes or
  reconstructed skin would be closer. The in vivo study partly offsets this.

### Free extracts, TAC after UV (3.4, p. 8, Figure 4)

- **No significant difference** at any concentration, and açai and Trolox were not significant either.
- **Limitation:** the TAC assay misses fast or pathway-specific changes in ROS (reactive oxygen species). No
  **DCFH-DA** ROS assay was done.

### Why 10 mg/mL (3.5, p. 9)

- 10 mg/mL was used as a **high-dose screening level**, to make the assay more sensitive and to compare free extract
  and emulsion at the **same nominal active-equivalent concentration**.
- mg/mL in culture medium is **not** the same as real use on skin, which is measured in mg/cm² and depends on how long
  the product stays on, the skin barrier and delivery.
- Lower cytotoxicity with emulsions reflects controlled bioavailability. It is **not proof of absolute safety**.

### Formulations, MTS (3.6, p. 9, Figure 5)

- **Free extracts at 10 mg/mL:** beer, hops and spent grain **reduced viability**, showing concentration-dependent
  cytotoxicity.
- **The text says** all emulsions significantly raised viability compared with untreated cells and with the free
  extracts.
- **Figure 5 shows** asterisks (p < 0.05) only for **Cream W.F. (the plain vehicle), beer, yeast and spent grain**.
  Wort and hops have none.
- **Biggest effect:** the **yeast cream** (about 1.2 AU).
- **Interpretation:** the vehicle has a **protective and modulating role**.

### Formulations, TAC (3.7, p. 10, Figure 6)

- **Free extracts at 10 mg/mL:** not significant.
- **Beer and yeast emulsions** significantly increased TAC compared with untreated cells and Emulsion WE. The other
  emulsions showed a non-significant upward trend.
- **UV control:** UV alone significantly lowered TAC (about 4.0 down to 3.1 AU).
- **Why the emulsion works better:**
  - **Free extract:** immediate full exposure, poor uptake, breakdown outside the cell, and non-specific interactions
    with the medium.
  - **Emulsion:** acts as a **reservoir** with **controlled release**, better solubilisation of amphiphilic compounds,
    and less acute stress on the cells.
- **Caveat:** TAC measures the cell's **global antioxidant buffering**, not direct radical scavenging.

### Stability and safety (3.8, pp. 11–13)

- **Table 2:** after centrifugation, 6 months at 4 °C, 6 months at 40 °C and 12 months at room temperature:

  | Parameter | Range | Two-way ANOVA result |
  |---|---|---|
  | **pH** | **5.7–6.2** | Storage effect not significant (F(3,48) = 1.961, p = 0.132). Formulation effect significant (p = 0.001), from baseline differences between matrices. |
  | **Density** | **0.95–0.99 g/mL** | Storage effect not significant (p = 0.854). Formulation effect significant (p = 0.031); e.g. hop is lower. |
  | **Viscosity** | **4750–5150 mPa·s** | Not significant for storage (p = 0.835), formulation (p = 0.354) or interaction (p = 0.9998). |

  The largest pH change was **yeast at 12 months at room temperature, 6.0 → 5.7**.
- **Colour (Table 3):** **ΔE\*ab ≤ 1.1 after 3 months**, which is below the threshold the eye can detect.

  | Emulsion | ΔE\*ab |
  |---|---|
  | WE | 0.33 |
  | Beer | 1.04 |
  | Wort | 1.02 |
  | Hop | 0.88 |
  | Yeast | 1.01 |
  | Spent grain | 0.22 |

- **Challenge test:** **all emulsions passed**, so the preservative works.
- **Patch test:** **no erythema, oedema or irritation**, so all were well tolerated.

### In vivo efficacy (3.9, pp. 13–14, Table 4; n = 50; mean ± SD)

Percentage changes were calculated from Table 4.

| Emulsion | TEWL (mg/m²/h) T0 → T30 | Hydration (%) T0 → T30 | Elasticity (N/m) T0 → T30 |
|---|---|---|---|
| WE (vehicle) | 15.00 ± 4.66 → 14.30 ± 1.18 (−4.7%) | 32.01 → 34.25 (+7.0%) | 60.12 → 66.19 (+10.1%) |
| **Beer (Emu1)** | **16.22 ± 5.12 → 10.77 ± 2.22 (−33.6%)** | **35.15 ± 5.93 → 42.26 ± 3.78 (+20.2%)** | **62.33 ± 3.27 → 70.24 ± 2.12 (+12.7%)** |
| Wort (Emu2) | 17.24 → 16.22 (−5.9%) | 34.28 → 38.44 (+12.1%) | 60.66 → 62.67 (+3.3%) |
| Hop (Emu3) | 15.70 → 14.29 (−9.0%) | 35.11 → 38.28 (+9.0%) | 68.24 → 65.44 (**−4.1%**) |
| **Yeast (Emu4)** | **16.29 ± 5.66 → 10.18 ± 1.08 (−37.5%)** | **33.27 ± 4.87 → 42.92 ± 2.48 (+29.0%)** | **61.21 ± 4.72 → 72.13 ± 5.55 (+17.8%)** |
| **Spent grain (Emu5)** | **14.45 ± 4.34 → 11.66 ± 2.28 (−19.3%)** | **34.22 ± 5.19 → 41.16 ± 3.17 (+20.3%)** | 64.33 ± 5.84 → 68.81 ± 6.92 (+7.0%) |

- **Baseline (T0) values were comparable** across treated areas, which the authors say confirms the design is
  reliable.
- **TEWL:** beer, yeast and spent grain gave a **significant reduction, about one-third**. WE and hops showed no
  significant change.
- **Hydration:** beer, yeast and spent grain gave a significant increase.
- **Elasticity:** the same three gave the biggest increase.
- **Discussion summary (p. 15):** TEWL fell by **20–38%** and hydration rose by about **20–29%**, which is within
  published benchmark ranges and so counts as a meaningful cosmetic improvement.
- **Interpretation:** lower TEWL means a **stronger stratum corneum barrier**.
- **Units:** the abstract gives TEWL in mg·m⁻²·h⁻¹, while the Table 4 header says mg/m²/h.

### In vitro–in vivo concordance (3.10, p. 14)

- **Method:** Spearman correlation of the combined MTS/TAC score against in vivo change, **one value per
  formulation**. The effective sample size is therefore the number of formulations, not 50.
- **Results:**

  | In vivo measure | Spearman ρ | p | Significant? |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | **Elasticity** | **≈ 0.94** | ≈ 0.0048 | Yes |
  | **Hydration** | **≈ 0.83** | ≈ 0.042 | Yes |
  | **TEWL** | **≈ 0.77** | — | No (trend only) |

- **Overall ranking:** yeast (Emu4) and beer (Emu1) were best both in vitro and in vivo. Wort and hops did little
  beyond the vehicle.
- **Caveat:** these findings are exploratory.

## 4. Discussion (pp. 14–16)

- Free extracts up to 1 mg/mL caused no change in viability, TAC or apoptosis proteins. This **confirms a safety
  profile**, and polyphenols often show weak activity on their own in resilient fibroblasts.
- **Antioxidant activity in chemical assays does not necessarily carry over to cells**, because of poor uptake,
  instability or rapid metabolism. This shows the **limits of testing extracts on their own** and the **added value of
  formulation design**.
- **Key finding:** at 10 mg/mL, putting the extracts into an emulsion **fully prevented cell death**. Emu1 and Emu4
  enhanced viability and TAC.
- **Proposed mechanisms (all stated to be hypothetical or inferential):**
  - controlled release (a reservoir effect)
  - less cellular exposure
  - synergy with emollients and emulsifiers
  - partitioning between the dispersed and continuous phases
  - better solubilisation and penetration through skin lipids
- **Vehicle effect:** Emulsion WE had moderate activity of its own, because cosmetic bases supply lipids and humectants.
  Even so, **Emu1 and Emu4 beat the blank**, so the effect is **not due to the vehicle alone**.
- **Active components:**
  - **Yeast:** β-glucans, peptides and minerals, which support healing and antioxidant defences.
  - **Beer:** phenolic acids and fermentation metabolites.
- **Strength:** **the in vivo results corroborate the in vitro results.** TEWL, hydration and elasticity are reliable
  indicators of skin health and anti-aging.
- **Not measured:** **skin permeation was not assessed directly.**

## 5. Conclusions (p. 16)

- Beer and selected by-products can be **sustainable cosmetic ingredients, *provided* they are delivered in an
  appropriate formulation**.
- **Free extracts (0.25–1 mg/mL)** were cytocompatible but had no significant effect.
- **O/W emulsions (20%) at 10 mg/mL** increased metabolic activity and antioxidant response and reduced cytotoxicity.
- **Product quality:**
  - stable under accelerated and long-term storage
  - meets **ISO 11930**
  - excellent tolerability in the patch test
- **In vivo (50 volunteers, 30 days):** beer, yeast and spent grain lowered TEWL and increased hydration and elasticity.
- **Efficacy depends on formulation as well as the extract** (bioavailability and exposure kinetics), in line with the
  **circular economy**.
- **Future work:**
  - **follow-up after treatment ends**, to see whether the benefits last
  - **mechanistic endpoints**: penetration, uptake and pathway-specific oxidative-stress markers

## Limitations stated by the authors

1. A **single immortalised fibroblast line** (BJ-5ta) does not reflect the complexity of skin (p. 8).
2. **TAC only**, with no ROS-specific assay such as **DCFH-DA** (p. 9).
3. **mg/mL in culture is not the same as real topical exposure in mg/cm²** (p. 9).
4. Lower emulsion cytotoxicity is **not proof of absolute safety** (p. 9).
5. The **mechanisms are hypothetical**, and permeation and uptake were not measured (pp. 15–16).
6. The **concordance analysis is exploratory**, with n = the number of formulations (p. 14).
7. There was **no follow-up after treatment** (p. 16).

## Inconsistencies in the article (useful for critical-appraisal questions)

- **Unclear wording in the abstract on elasticity:** "beer and yeast formulations (62.33→70.24) and yeast
  (61.21→72.13)". From Table 4, the first pair is beer and the second is yeast. Spent grain elasticity (64.33→68.81)
  is not given in the abstract.
- **Table 4 legend doesn't match the methods:**
  - The legend says "n = 50 independent experiments" and "two-way ANOVA (treatment × concentration) + Dunnett".
  - The methods say n = 50 subjects, two-way repeated-measures ANOVA (treatment × time), Holm–Bonferroni.
  - Table 4 gives **no p-values**, so the claim of significance cannot be checked from the table.
- **Statistics for the in vitro work:** the methods say one-way ANOVA, but the figure legends say two-way ANOVA.
- **Figure 5 vs text:** the text says all formulations significantly increased viability, but the figure shows no
  asterisk for the wort and hop creams. The text also mentions "WF", which is never defined.
- **Western blot / apoptosis proteins:** these appear in Figure 1 and the discussion, but no Western blot method or
  result is reported.
- **pH described as "close to neutrality"** even though 5.7–6.2 is slightly acidic.
- **Colour stability:** measured only up to **3 months**, while other stability tests ran for up to 12 months.
- **Conflict of interest:** two authors work for Recusol Srl, yet the authors declare no conflict of interest.
- **Reference list:** some numbers don't match the text. For example, [18] is cited as the group's earlier beer study,
  but the reference list gives an unrelated hydration paper.

## Research-methods classification (links to Section 3.1)

- **Overall:** a **quantitative, experimental** study combining **in vitro lab assays and an in vivo controlled clinical
  trial**.
- **No qualitative component:** there are no questionnaires about how the products felt.
- **Design features:**
  - **Randomised, double-blind, vehicle-controlled** (Emulsion WE).
  - **Within-subject comparison:** split-forearm, with a pre/post (T0 vs T30) comparison.
- **Instruments:** validated, non-invasive bio-instruments.
- **Statistics:** ANOVA, post-hoc tests and Spearman correlation, with p < 0.05.
