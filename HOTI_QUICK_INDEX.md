### In vitro results

| Question topic | Key answer | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Free extracts, MTS | No significant change at any concentration; no toxicity → cytocompatible | 3.3, p.8 |
| Free extracts, TAC | No significant difference after UV, even with açai or Trolox (text) | 3.4, p.8 |
| Free extracts at 10 mg/mL | Beer, hops and spent grain **reduced viability** (dose-dependent toxicity) | 3.6, p.9 |
| Creams, MTS | Creams raised viability; beer, yeast and spent grain significant; yeast highest (Fig. 5) | 3.6, pp.9–10 |
| Creams, TAC | **Beer and yeast** creams significantly increased TAC vs untreated and WE; others a non-significant trend | 3.7, p.10 |
| Why creams work better | Reservoir effect, controlled release, better solubilisation, less acute cell stress; free extracts are poorly taken up and broken down quickly | 3.7, pp.10–11; 4, pp.15–16 |
| What TAC measures | "global intracellular antioxidant buffering capacity rather than direct radical scavenging" | 3.7, p.11 |

### Discussion, conclusions and limitations

| Question topic | Key answer | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Main conclusion | Brewing products are sustainable cosmetic ingredients "provided they are incorporated through appropriate delivery formulations" | 5, p.16 |
| Vehicle or active? | WE had moderate activity of its own, but "Emu 1 and 4 consistently outperformed the blank emulsion… cannot be attributed to the vehicle alone" | 4, p.15 |
| Active components | Yeast: β-glucans, peptides, minerals. Beer: phenolic acids and fermentation metabolites | 4, p.15 |
| Chemical assays vs cells | Antioxidant activity in chemical assays "do[es] not necessarily translate into cellular efficacy" (poor uptake, instability, metabolism) | 4, p.14 |
| Key strength | In vivo results "strongly corroborate the in vitro findings" | 4, p.15 |
| Limitations | One immortalised cell line (p.8); TAC only, no DCFH-DA (p.9); mg/mL ≠ mg/cm² (p.9); not proof of absolute safety (p.9); mechanisms hypothetical, skin permeation not measured (pp.15–16); exploratory concordance (p.14) | as listed |
| Future research | Follow-up after treatment ends; mechanistic endpoints (penetration, uptake, pathway-specific oxidative-stress markers) | 5, p.16 |
| Funding and conflicts | No external funding; two authors employed by Recusol Srl, no commercial conflict declared | pp.16–17 |
| Research-method type | Quantitative, experimental: in vitro lab assays + randomised, double-blind, vehicle-controlled in vivo trial (within-subject, before/after); no qualitative part | whole paper |
